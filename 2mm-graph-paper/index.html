Five squares make a centimetre, so the bold line every fifth square gives 10 mm blocks. The counts below are large enough to be worth stating plainly: an A4 sheet carries over twelve thousand squares and around four hundred and fifty printed lines.

What a 2 mm grid is for

Every use here has the same shape: the grid needs to be fine enough to read a value off, and quiet enough to disappear under the work.

Lab and scientific plotting

Fine enough to place a point precisely and to interpolate between gridlines by eye, which is the whole reason fine graph paper exists.

Reading values off a curve

With 5 squares to the centimetre, an intermediate reading can be estimated to a fifth of a small division without guessing.

Fine technical drawing

Detail views and sections where a 5 mm grid would be too coarse to place anything accurately.

Scale modelling

At 1:50, one square is 10 cm of real length. At 1:100 it is 20 cm. Useful for laying out small components without redrawing a scale each time.

Detailed craft charts

Beadwork, fine embroidery and miniature knitting charts where a coarser grid would not hold enough cells to describe the piece.

A quiet background for ink work

Set the colour to grey and the grid guides the hand without competing with the drawing, which the default blue will do at this density.

Before you print

Scaling hurts most at this density: a page shrunk by five percent turns a 2 mm square into 1.9 mm, and the accumulated error across 95 columns is nearly a centimetre. Set the scale to 100% or Actual Size, then check the 100 mm bar — it should span exactly 50 squares. The full setting-by-setting guide is on the home page.

Questions about 2 mm grids

Is 2 mm graph paper the same as millimetre paper?

Not quite. Millimetre paper normally means a 1 mm grid with heavier lines every 5 and 10 mm, which is finer again. A 2 mm grid is the practical middle ground: fine enough to interpolate on, coarse enough that a home printer reproduces every line cleanly.

Will my printer actually resolve a 2 mm grid?

A laser printer will, comfortably. An inkjet on plain paper may thicken the lines slightly, which at this density reads as a tint across the page rather than as individual lines. If that happens, switch the line colour to grey, which is the setting that fixes it.

How many 2 mm squares fit on a page?

On A4 with the measuring bars on, 95 across by 129 down — over twelve thousand squares. Letter gives 97 by 120. Turning the bars off adds nine more rows on either paper.

Why is this PDF larger than the other sizes?

Because it contains far more lines. A 2 mm A4 sheet is around 450 vector lines against roughly 70 for a quarter inch sheet, so the file is a few kilobytes bigger. It is still a fraction of the size of any image-based grid, and it stays perfectly sharp at any zoom.

Is 2 mm the same as 1/10 inch?

No. A tenth of an inch is 2.54 mm, 27 percent wider. On a grid this fine the two look identical side by side and diverge badly over a full sheet, so it is worth checking which one a template or chart actually assumes.

What line colour works best at 2 mm?

Grey or the default blue. Black turns the sheet into a solid grey block from any distance and makes pencil work hard to distinguish from the grid. Red and green are legible but tiring to work on at this density.
