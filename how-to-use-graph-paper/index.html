How to use graph paper

Graph paper is a page ruled into equal squares. Each square stands for a fixed amount, whether that is one unit on a graph, one foot of a room or one stitch of a pattern, so you can count instead of measure. Here is how to use it for graphs, scale drawings, sketching and everyday math.

How to draw a graph on graph paper

Worked example: plot y = 2x + 1 for x from −3 to 3. A 1 cm or 1/4 inch grid works well, or start from coordinate graph paper with the axes already drawn.

1. Make a table of values

Work out a few points first: (−3, −5), (−1, −1), (0, 1), (1, 3), (3, 7). The table tells you how far the axes need to reach.

2. Draw the axes on a grid line

Put the x-axis and y-axis along existing lines, not between them. Leave enough squares on each side for the smallest and largest values: here −3 to 3 across and −5 to 7 up.

3. Choose a scale and label it

Decide what one square is worth, for example 1 unit per square. Use the same step all the way along each axis, write the numbers on the lines, and name each axis.

4. Plot and join the points

Count across for x, then up or down for y, and mark each point with a small cross. Join them with a ruler for a straight line or a smooth freehand curve for anything else, then give the graph a title.

Picking a scale: divide the range of your data by the number of squares you have, then round up to an easy step such as 1, 2, 5 or 10. Data from 0 to 170 on an axis 20 squares long needs at least 8.5 per square, so use 10.

How to use graph paper to draw to scale

A scale drawing turns real measurements into squares. Choose the scale first, then convert every measurement the same way.

Pick the scale

The classic US floor plan scale is 1/4 inch to 1 foot, so on 1/4 inch graph paper one square is one foot. In metric, 1:100 on 1 cm paper makes one square one metre.

Convert by counting

A 12 × 15 ft room at one square per foot is 12 × 15 squares, which measures 3 × 3.75 inches on the page. Round odd measurements to the nearest half square.

Write the scale down

Note it on the sheet, for example “1 square = 1 ft”. Anyone reading the plan later, including you, needs it to read the sizes back.

Print it true to size

A scale drawing is only as accurate as the paper. Print at 100% and check the measuring bar, as described in how to print graph paper.

How to use graph paper for drawing

Artists use a grid to copy proportions accurately, and designers use it to keep shapes square.

Grid method

Draw a grid over a reference picture and a matching grid on your paper, then copy one square at a time. To enlarge, make the paper grid bigger: a 1 cm grid on the photo and a 2 cm grid on the paper doubles the size.

Pixel art and patterns

One square is one pixel, stitch or bead. Smaller grids like 1/8 inch fit bigger designs; see graph paper for pixel art.

3D sketches

For objects drawn at an angle, use isometric paper; its lines already run at 30°, so cubes and boxes come out right without measuring.

Lettering and logos

Use the squares to keep letter height, spacing and baselines even. Light dot grid paper gives the same guidance with less visual noise.

How to use graph paper for math

Keep columns aligned

Write one digit per square in long multiplication and long division. Place values stay lined up, which removes a whole class of mistakes.

Area and perimeter

Draw shapes along the lines and count: each whole square is one square unit, and each edge is one unit of perimeter.

Coordinates and transformations

Plot points, reflect shapes across an axis and translate them by counting squares. Coordinate paper saves drawing the axes.

Fractions and bar models

A strip of 10 or 12 squares makes fractions visible: shade 3 of 12 squares and it is easy to see that it equals 1/4.

Questions about using graph paper

What is graph paper?

Paper printed with a grid of equal squares, also called grid paper, squared paper or quad paper. The squares let you plot, draw and measure by counting instead of using a ruler every time.

What is graph paper used for?

Plotting graphs and charts, geometry and coordinate work, keeping long calculations aligned, floor plans and other scale drawings, engineering sketches, and craft patterns such as cross stitch, knitting and pixel art.

How do you write on graph paper?

For notes, ignore the vertical lines and use each row as a line of text. For math, put one digit or symbol in each square so columns stay aligned. Larger squares such as 1 cm suit beginners.

Do I need to start the axes in the middle of the page?

Only if you have negative values. If all your data is positive, put the origin near the bottom-left corner so the whole page is used for the graph.
